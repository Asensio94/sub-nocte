Did the published forecasts hold up?

Generated on 14 September 2026. Every number here comes from forecasts that were published before the night happened.

Where the forecasts come from. The daily routine commits the forecast in force, so the history of this repository is an archive of forecasts stamped with the day they were issued. Nothing is re-run: the model is judged on what it actually said. Against it goes the density each city's nearest radar went on to measure, taken again from the public archive, which publishes each day with about two days of delay.

How to read it. The rank correlation asks whether the busier nights of a place were forecast as the busier ones: it needs no long radar record, so it is the number that holds everywhere. The alert figures only use the 210 cases where the radar has at least 100 nights in its own record, because without that there is nothing to call a busy night against; chance alone would land on one 36% of the time. The size is the honest weak point: the model orders nights better than it measures them.

What this cannot tell you

  • The radar is not in the city: it is the nearest one, tens of kilometres away, and it sees a circle of its own. A forecast can be right about the city and still miss the radar.
  • The Spanish radars renewed in 2025 have no full autumn in the archive yet, so what counts as a busy night there is not settled and their alert figures are left out of the totals.
  • The French radars changed scale in 2019, by a factor of tens: their nights are comparable with each other from that year on, but not with the ones before.
  • Only cities whose nearest radar still publishes can be checked at all.
